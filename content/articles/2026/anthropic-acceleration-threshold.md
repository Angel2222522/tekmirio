Companies heading for a stock market listing usually let their sales figures do the talking. Anthropic, the San Francisco company behind the Claude AI models, has put something stranger next to its numbers: a forecast that its next models may soon reach the point where AI dramatically accelerates the development of AI itself.

The language appears in an amended prospectus (Form S-1/A) that the company filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) on Monday, 2 November 2026. The first version of the prospectus was filed on 26 October.

## What the filing says

In the section on risk factors, Anthropic writes: "our internal estimates and our expectations regarding future models, rather than our current models, indicate that we may imminently meet our Responsible Scaling Policy's definition of 'dramatically accelerated progress attributable to AI.'"

It immediately draws a line around that statement: "We have not designated any of our current models as exceeding this threshold, and we continue to assess the underlying data."

The company also concedes that measuring its own speed is hard. "We have in the past not identified, and may in the future not identify, a significant acceleration in our rate of development until after it has occurred," the filing says. Anthropic adds that it is evaluating what meeting the threshold would mean and will publish further reports, as its policy requires.

## What the threshold means

The Responsible Scaling Policy is Anthropic's internal rulebook, tying what each model can do to the safety measures the company must have in place. The current version, 3.4, took effect on 8 July 2026 and includes a threshold for automated research and development.

That threshold can be met in one of two ways. The first is if models could fully stand in for the company's entire staff of research scientists and research engineers at competitive cost. The second, which is the one the prospectus points to, is "dramatic acceleration": the company observes or expects progress in overall AI capabilities to run at double the usual rate, with the doubling plausibly driven to a substantial degree by automated research and engineering.

"Double the rate" has a precise meaning in the policy: as much progress in one year as would normally take two, measured over at least three model generations. The policy offers an example. If a normal year brought a ninefold "effective scaleup", doubling the rate would mean something like 81-fold. It stresses that this is not the same as researchers simply working twice as fast.

In wider debate, the idea is known as recursive self-improvement, a term the prospectus itself uses. "Using AI to help build more capable AI could also pose a considerable safety risk, particularly if it sharply accelerates the pace of progress," it says.

## Humans in the loop

The filing does not describe a company planning to let its models build their successors unsupervised. "We do not anticipate being able to safely use Claude to fully autonomously design and build successive generations of frontier models without meaningful human supervision or understanding, if and when that becomes technically possible," it states. Anthropic says it is therefore considering changes to its policy "to keep humans in control of frontier model development at Anthropic."

It also offers a recent example of slowing down: the company says it delayed pre-training a new frontier base model by more than a month to strengthen security measures against the theft of model weights.

At the same time, the prospectus presents Claude's use inside the company as a competitive edge. It calls the cycle in which each model helps build the next "closing the loop", and warns that if it under-invests, it risks "falling progressively further behind" rivals.

## The numbers

The financial figures show why the listing is drawing so much attention:

- Revenue reached $17.0 billion in the July–September 2026 quarter, up from $197 million in the last quarter of 2024.
- Quarterly revenue grew 1,127% year on year, while compute and infrastructure expenses grew 382%.
- Headcount stood at about 5,300 on 30 September 2026, up from about 940 at the end of 2024.

Anthropic is a public benefit corporation. Its chief executive and co-founder is Dario Amodei. The company has applied to list its shares on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker "ANT". The price range has not yet been filled in in the amended filing.

The warning about the threshold sits among the risk factors, the part of a prospectus where companies list what could go wrong. Two questions now come into focus: whether Anthropic will in fact change its policy to keep people in control, and when it will first designate one of its models as having crossed the line.
